01

The open circle, and the point beyond

Every element of this identity is built from a single thought: the self is not a solved problem.

The mark begins where the practice begins — with the ensō, the circle of Zen calligraphy, drawn here not in ink but in geometry: two circles, one displaced, their difference a single tapering stroke. It never closes. What it holds stays partly unknowable, and the brand does not pretend otherwise.

At the opening, one point stands apart from the circumference. It is the datum outside the curve. The patient whose chart says fine and whose body says otherwise. The outlier — named, not averaged away.

Together the two forms read as an O, as a monogram, and as a diagnosis: the whole is known by the point that departs from it.

Current — Two strokes, closed
→
Proposed — One stroke, open

An evolution, not a replacement. The olive circle your patients already recognise is kept — then reduced to one stroke, opened, and given its point. One colour, one form: it survives an embroidered polo, a fax-grade photocopy, and a 16-pixel favicon without losing its name.

02

Drawn by compass, not by hand

Nothing in the mark is freehand. Two circles generate the stroke; a 45° axis through their centres places the point. Any studio in the world can redraw it from four numbers — the property of every logo that has lasted fifty years.

  • Outer circler = 100 units, the boundary of what is measured
  • Inner circler = 88, displaced 14 units toward the opening
  • Aperture59.5°, oriented northeast — rising, not falling
  • The pointr = 10.5, set on the 45° axis, outside the circle
  • Clear spaceHalf the mark's height on all sides
  • Minimum size16 px digital / 8 mm print

Optima — Wordmark & Utility
Hermann Zapf, 1958. A sans with the memory of a chisel — clinical enough for a chart, classical enough for a temple. Set in tracked capitals, it is the voice of the wordmark.

Both faces are by one designer, drawn nine years apart — the serif carries the mystery, the sans carries the medicine, and they agree at the skeleton. That coherence is why the system will not need a refresh in 2036.

07

Positioning

Outlier Medicine practices for the people the system averages out — rigor in service of the unmeasured self.

The corporate audience does not need convincing that they are stressed; they need permission to take the unquantified parts of themselves seriously, delivered in a visual language as credible as their cardiologist's. This identity gives Eastern medicine the typographic and formal authority of a private bank — so that self-mystery reads not as mysticism, but as the honest edge of knowledge.

Precise
Numbers, named forms, no vagueness. The geometry is published because nothing is hidden.
Unhurried
Whitespace is the brand's loudest gesture. Nothing shouts; the point simply stands apart.
Reverent
The ensō is borrowed with respect, then earned with craft — never costume, never trend.
Direct
Speaks to the patient as a peer: your chart says fine, you know better.
