Outlier Medicine — Identity, Round Two

The double stroke returns

Fair verdict on round one: a lone tapered crescent with a point beside it borrows the silhouette of a flag, and no amount of rationale untangles a silhouette. So every direction below keeps the double stroke from your original mark — as a closed swirl or an open pair — so the mass always reads as a ring, never a moon.

Olive stays. Beyond the baseline stays — it says in corporate language what you mean by life beyond the basic minimal requirement. No deck this time: six marks, each with its small-size test and reversed version. React to what pulls; we iterate from there and build the brand book around the winner.

01Two Strokes, Refined

Your current mark, mastered. Both interlocking strokes are kept and redrawn on compass geometry, so each taper is exact and the seam between them is deliberate. Moss leads, Sage follows. The ring closes; the swirl carries the motion.

Scale test
02The Open Pair

Two strokes, two openings. Neither stroke closes the circle alone — together they almost do. The double aperture kills the crescent read outright, and the openness carries the self-mystery: what the circle holds stays partly unknowable.

Scale test
03The Still Point

The open pair, with a single point at dead center. Here the outlier is not outside the circle — it is the self the circle is drawn around. Reads as focus, breath, a pupil. The quietest of the six.

Scale test
04The Orbit

The full double-stroke ring with the point set beyond it. Because the ring is closed, the pair reads as mass and satellite — an orbit, not a flag. The most literal outlier of the set; keep or kill the dot after seeing 01.

Scale test
05The Departed Stroke

No dot at all. The two strokes share a circle; one has stepped off it. The outlier is not a point outside the system — it is part of the whole, moved. The most conceptual direction, and the hardest to mistake for anything else.

Scale test
06The Twelve

The TCM body clock: twelve meridians, twelve positions on the circle. Eleven hold. One stands apart. Corporate audiences read data; patients read the mala. Weakest at favicon size — shown honestly below.
